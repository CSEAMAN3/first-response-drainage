Most people associate prolonged hot weather with dry gardens, brown lawns and hosepipe bans.

However, periods of extended drought can also have a surprising effect on older drainage systems.

As the ground dries out, it naturally shrinks and moves. For modern drainage systems this is rarely a problem, but older clay drainage pipes can be more vulnerable to movement, cracking and joint separation.

In this guide, we'll explain how summer drought can affect your drains, the warning signs to look out for and what you can do if you suspect there's a problem.

---

## Why Does Dry Weather Affect Drains?

It might seem strange that a lack of rain can damage underground pipes, but the answer lies in the soil surrounding them.

Many parts of Norfolk, Suffolk and Cambridgeshire contain clay-rich soils. Clay expands when it's wet and shrinks as it dries.

During prolonged periods of hot weather, this shrinkage can cause slight ground movement around underground drainage pipes.

Older drainage systems—particularly clay pipework installed decades ago—can become vulnerable to:

- Cracked pipes
- Joint separation
- Minor displacement
- Increased water infiltration
- Structural weakness

These issues often develop gradually rather than overnight.

---

## Older Drainage Systems Are More Vulnerable

Many older properties still rely on original clay drainage pipes.

While these pipes were built to last, decades of ground movement, tree root growth and changing weather conditions can take their toll.

A prolonged dry spell may expose weaknesses that have developed over many years.

If left untreated, minor defects can eventually lead to recurring blockages or more extensive drainage repairs.

---

## Tree Roots Become More Aggressive During Drought

Trees naturally seek moisture.

During dry weather, roots often grow further in search of water.

If an older drain already has small cracks or gaps between pipe joints, tree roots can enter the drainage system and continue growing inside the pipe.

Over time this can:

- Restrict water flow
- Trap debris
- Cause recurring blockages
- Lead to structural damage

## Why Problems Often Appear After Heavy Rain

One of the most common situations we encounter is homeowners experiencing drainage problems shortly after the first heavy rainfall following a prolonged dry period.

This isn't usually because the rain caused the damage.

Instead, the drought may have already weakened the drainage system.

When heavy rainfall finally arrives, the additional volume of water flowing through the drains can expose existing defects that previously went unnoticed.

This is why some drainage issues seem to appear "all of a sudden" after weeks of dry weather.

---

## Signs Your Drainage System May Have Been Affected

Not every drainage problem is obvious straight away.

Some early warning signs include:

- Slow-draining sinks or showers
- Repeated blocked drains
- Bad smells around outside drains
- Overflowing inspection chambers
- Damp patches appearing unexpectedly
- Sunken ground around drainage runs

If you notice any of these signs, it's worth arranging a professional inspection before the problem becomes more serious.

## FAQs

### Can hot weather really damage drains?

Hot weather itself doesn't damage drains, but prolonged dry conditions can cause the surrounding ground to shrink and move. Older drainage systems may be more vulnerable to this movement.

---

### Are clay drainage pipes more likely to crack?

Yes. Older clay pipes can become more susceptible to cracking or joint separation as the ground moves over time, particularly during extended periods of drought.

---

### Should I have my drains inspected after a drought?

If you've noticed recurring blockages, bad smells or slow drainage following a prolonged dry spell, a CCTV drainage survey can identify any underlying problems before they become more serious.

---

### Can tree roots damage drains during dry weather?

Yes. Trees search for moisture during drought conditions, and roots can exploit small cracks or gaps in older drainage pipes, eventually causing blockages or structural damage.
